# Denial Prevention Simulator

What control should happen *before* a claim goes out, so avoidable denials never enter the
work queue?

Most denial reporting is a post-mortem: here are last month's top denial reasons, go work
them. This project moves the decision earlier. It scores unsubmitted claims on preventable
denial risk, maps each failure to a named prevention rule and owner, and separately ranks
already-denied claims by recovery priority so the appeals queue is worked in the order that
matters.

Fictional organization: **Northbridge Medical Group**. All data is synthetic, generated by
`src/generate_data.py`.

---

## The business problem

Northbridge runs a denial rate around 14% and works denials reactively — the notice arrives,
someone opens a queue, someone appeals. Nobody can answer two questions: which of those
denials were preventable at the front end, and which unsubmitted claims are about to become
next month's denials.

## Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | What they need |
|---|---|
| Revenue cycle director | Denial rate, denied dollars, preventable share, trend |
| Billing manager | Pre-submission queue with a clear action per claim |
| Coding lead | Claims failing code validation before submission |
| Authorization staff | Claims held for missing or mismatched authorization |
| Payer relations | Which payers and reasons concentrate denied dollars |
| Analysts | Rule definitions, score logic, data-quality log |

## Scope and assumptions

In scope: professional and outpatient claims with service dates 2026-01-01 to 2026-08-31,
plus a live pipeline of 900 unsubmitted claims; pre-submission controls, denial reasons,
resubmission and recovery.

Out of scope: contract modeling, fee schedule analysis, patient responsibility, collections,
clinical coding accuracy itself.

Assumptions:
- One denial reason per claim, the primary reason.
- Authorization is required by procedure group, not by individual payer policy.
- A claim is "preventable" when its denial reason maps to a pre-submission control.
- Recovery likelihood is a fixed assumption (75% preventable, 30% not), not a fitted model.

## Data source statement

Fully synthetic, fixed seed, generated locally. No employer data, no patient data, no PHI,

| Table | Grain | Rows |

## Prevention rules

| Rule | Trigger | Prevention action | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| PR-01 | Authorization required and not on file | Hold claim, verify authorization | Authorization team |
| PR-02 | Eligibility not verified | Verify active coverage for the date of service | Billing intake |
| PR-03 | Documentation incomplete | Request missing clinical documentation | Clinical documentation |
| PR-04 | Coding validation failed | Route to coding review before submission | Coding team |
| PR-05 | More than 20 days since date of service | Expedite submission review | Billing supervisor |

Rules are data, not code — they live in `prevention_rules.csv` so thresholds can change
without touching the pipeline.

## The three scores

```
Preventable Denial Risk = 30 x (authorization required and missing)
                        + 20 x (eligibility not verified)
                        + 20 x (documentation incomplete)
                        + 15 x (coding validation failed)
                        + 15 x (high timely filing risk)

Risk bands: Low 0-14, Medium 15-29, High 30+

Recovery Priority = 0.45 x denied-dollar index
                  + 0.30 x recovery likelihood
                  + 0.15 x filing urgency
                  + 0.10 x claim age risk

Prevention Opportunity = denied amount x preventable probability
```

All three are additive and explainable on purpose. A billing supervisor has to be able to
look at a score of 50 and see exactly which two checks failed.

## Data quality approach

`src/clean_and_score.py` runs 10 rules: duplicate claim IDs, denied claims with no reason
code, denied amount exceeding billed amount, non-positive charges, submission before service
date, invalid status, status conflicting with the denial flag, payer and reason reference
integrity, and recovery exceeding the denied amount. Critical failures are excluded from
reporting and logged rather than silently corrected.

Last run: 12,014 rows in, 60 exceptions, 33 records blocked, **Data Quality Score 99.51%**.

## Findings

From the synthetic dataset in this repo. Modeled scenario only.

1. **Most denied dollars trace to a pre-submission control.**
   Of $5.42M denied, $4.48M (82.7%) carried a reason that maps to one of the five prevention
   rules; by claim count the preventable share is 76.6%. Authorization alone accounts for $1.96M.

2. **Authorization is the single largest bucket.** 420 denied claims, $1.96M, all mapped to
   PR-01 or PR-02. The next largest category, plan coverage at $935K, is *not* preventable at
   the front end — worth naming, because it sets a ceiling on what prevention can achieve.

3. **The risk score separates outcomes.** Denial rate was 7.8% for Low-risk claims, 22.9%
   Medium, 37.8% High. The score is doing real work, not restating the answer.

4. **Late submission is disproportionately fatal.** Claims flagged high timely-filing risk
   denied at 45.7% versus 13.0% for low risk. Only 197 claims, but they are nearly coin-flips.

5. **Denied dollars concentrate in surgery.** Surgery is 13% of claim volume but $3.47M of
   $5.42M denied dollars, because the average charge is large. Volume-based denial reporting
   completely hides this.

6. **The live pipeline already contains the next wave.** 104 of 900 unsubmitted claims score
   High risk, carrying $505K in charges. At the historical High-risk denial rate that is
   about $191K of modeled denied exposure sitting in the queue right now.

## Control effectiveness simulation

| Intercept coverage | High-risk claims | Charges at risk | Modeled denied exposure | Modeled avoided exposure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | 104 | $504,650 | $190,551 | $95,275 |
| 70% | 104 | $504,650 | $190,551 | $133,386 |
| 90% | 104 | $504,650 | $190,551 | $171,496 |

Read this as exposure modeling, not a savings forecast. It assumes the intercept actually
fixes the underlying gap, which in practice it will not always do, and it uses the
historical High-risk denial rate as the probability. Both assumptions need validation
against production before a dollar figure is quoted to anyone.

## Recommendations

1. **Automate PR-01 as a hard hold.** Authorization is the largest preventable bucket and the
   cheapest check to run. Claims requiring auth do not leave the system without one.
2. **Put a 20-day submission alert on the billing supervisor's queue.** PR-05 currently holds
   379 pending claims and $908K — it is the highest-volume rule and the easiest to monitor.
3. **Route by risk band, not by claim type.** Low risk submits automatically, Medium gets a
   checklist, High goes to an owner-assigned exception queue.
4. **Work denials by recovery priority, not by date received.** Query 10 produces that queue.
5. **Report preventable and non-preventable denials separately.** Mixing coverage denials
   into a prevention metric makes the program look like it is failing when it is not.
6. **Feed denial outcomes back into the rule thresholds monthly.** Rules that never fire, or
   fire on everything, are noise.

No savings figure is claimed. All impact is stated as modeled opportunity subject to
validation.

## Limitations

- Synthetic data. The causal links between control failures and denials are ones I built in,
  so the analysis shows method rather than a real-world finding.
- Recovery likelihood is assumed, not modeled from historical appeal outcomes.
- One denial reason per claim; real remittances often carry several.
- No contract, fee schedule, or patient responsibility dimension.
- Score weights are unfitted. A logistic regression on production data would almost certainly
  reweight them, and that is the right next step.
- Power BI `.pbix` not committed; model, measures, and page specs are in
  [`docs/10_dashboard_spec.md`](docs/10_dashboard_spec.md).
